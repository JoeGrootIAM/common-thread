Before we suggest a business

People and community come first.

A business is one possible response—not the answer to every community need.

01

Listen before building

Ask the people affected what they need, what already exists, and whether a small enterprise would actually help.

02

Suggestions are hypotheses

A match is a starting point—not evidence of demand, income, legal compliance, safety, or community benefit.

03

Protect people and place

Respect consent, fair pay, privacy, local rules, and your own capacity. Pause or refer work outside your competence.

04

Grow only if it helps

Choose useful, durable work over growth at all costs. Revise or stop if the work harms people or isn’t sustainable.

The kind of success we believe in

A good business leaves a place stronger.

We’re choosing restorative business over growth at all costs: health, community, and longevity over extraction. The healthiest communities can feed, build, fix, and care for themselves.

True wealth can look like useful work, fair livelihoods, trusted relationships, and resources that stay close to home—not just a corporate exit.

That means keeping human skill in the loop and ownership within reach—not automating away relationships or concentrating the local market.
